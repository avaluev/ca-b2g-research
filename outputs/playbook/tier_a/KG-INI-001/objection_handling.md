# KG-INI-001 — Objection Handling

## Objection 1: "We don't know who you are — why should UDP accept pro-bono TOR help from an unknown vendor?"

**Response:** "We understand — and we designed the entry specifically to address this. We are already registered with WB Digital CASA KG PIU and ADB Project 55109 PIU. These are the donor institutions whose procurement standards the TORs must conform to. UDP can introduce us to their WB/ADB counterparts as a pre-cleared TA provider. Our track record on X-Road/Tunduk-class TOR drafting is verifiable through those donor channels. The TA offer is structured as a transparent, named engagement — not an anonymous consulting arrangement."

## Objection 2: "eGA Estonia is already our technical partner — why do we need you?"

**Response:** "We are specifically proposing to complement eGA, not replace them. eGA excels at the governance layer and X-Road technical architecture. What eGA cannot field is Russian-Kyrgyz bilingual AI/LLM capability and the Digital Code AI registry specification. These are genuinely different competencies. The strongest TOR package for Tunduk 2.0 is one where eGA authors the X-Road governance section and we author the AI inference fabric and registry section. We have already briefed the eGA KG advisor on this partnership framing — there is no conflict."

## Objection 3: "The structural regulations are not published yet — how can you draft TORs?"

**Response:** "This is exactly why the TOR drafting must start now, before the structural regulations are finalized. Standard practice in donor-funded governance transitions is to draft TORs in parallel with the regulatory process — the TORs are then updated when regulations finalize. Our draft TORs are explicitly framed as provisional, compliant with WB/ADB modalities, and versioned so updates take <1 week when final regulations land. Waiting for final regulations before drafting means the tender cycle starts 3 months later — which compresses the implementation window before donor program close dates."

## Objection 4: "We don't have budget for TA"

**Response:** "The pro-bono TOR drafting requires zero UDP budget. Three active donor TA budgets cover this: EU EGA twinning (already funded), WB Digital CASA TA component (already funded), ADB Project 55109 TA (already funded). We will structure our engagement to draw from these existing TA envelopes — UDP's role is simply to request us as the TA provider within the donor's procurement process. No new budget line needed."

## Objection 5: "What if the paid contracts go to someone else after we use your TORs?"

**Response:** "This is a legitimate risk and we are honest about it. Our pro-bono engagement is a relationship investment, not a contract guarantee. What TOR co-authorship gives us is: named co-author credit on published government documents, pre-qualification for tenders where we authored the scope, and demonstrated in-country track record with both donor PIUs and UDP. These are procurement advantages, not procurement locks. If UDP awards contracts to other vendors despite our TOR work, we will have failed at the relationship, not been cheated — and we will have learned something important about how UDP makes decisions."
