# INI-001 — Objection Handling

---

## Objection 1: "We already have preferred vendors for the Fund — criteria are an internal process"

**Why they raise it:** The Fund working group may have already informally consulted MTS AI or Yandex (before sanctions awareness), or domestic AI stakeholders (NUU, IT Park). The official posture is that criteria are a sovereign domestic process.

**Response:** "We are not asking to join the working group — we are submitting an independent technical brief as an outside voice, exactly as the UAE AI fund accepted input from Stanford HAI and Masdar Institute before finalizing Falcon's evaluation framework. We bring one specific asset no domestic actor has: a verified bilingual Uzbek-Russian LLM benchmark dataset, co-authored with Stanford HAI, that the working group can use as a reference point regardless of which vendor ultimately receives funding. We want to be helpful, not privileged."

**Tone note:** Frame as a contribution to the Fund's legitimacy, not as lobbying for your own project.

---

## Objection 2: "Foreign vendors are not eligible for the AI Fund"

**Why they raise it:** PP-320 criteria may be drafted with local-first or JV-mandatory rules. This is the primary regulatory risk.

**Response:** "We anticipated this and have structured our engagement as an IT Park resident JV with a named Uzbek development team from day one. IT Park residency under UZ-PP-2024-087 (digital export incentives) qualifies the JV as a local resident exporter. The flagship application we would submit is from the JV entity, not the foreign parent. We are happy to share our IT Park residency registration timeline now, so the Fund criteria can anticipate our JV structure."

**Evidence needed:** Draft IT Park JV registration documentation (have this ready before first contact).

---

## Objection 3: "Our AI Center team can train the model ourselves — we don't need a vendor"

**Why they raise it:** AI Center was established in 2025 and has a mandate to build domestic capability. This is a genuine institutional pride objection, especially from Toshmatov if he has a Skoltech / Google AI Residency background.

**Response:** "We agree — the long-term goal should be that the AI Center trains and maintains the model. What we offer is a faster path to the first flagship: a production-ready Russian-Uzbek bilingual base model that the AI Center's team can fine-tune, extend, and own. Our role is to deliver the base, co-publish the benchmark, and transfer the training methodology to your team under the JV. Year 2 and beyond, the AI Center runs the model independently. This is exactly how UAE TII structured Falcon — external methodology, domestic training team."

---

## Objection 4: "Show us a reference government that has deployed your stack"

**Why they raise it:** New vendor, new stack, sovereign-scale risk.

**Response:** "Our most direct government reference is [reference deployment — fill in before contact]. For the Uzbek-Russian bilingual layer specifically, we can demonstrate production-quality performance on the UZ government NLP test set we have built with NUU AI Cluster. We will co-publish that benchmark openly — you can run it yourself on any competing vendor's model. The benchmark is our proof of capability; the JV is our proof of commitment to UZ."

---

## Objection 5: "What happens if the Fund criteria favor larger vendors with more government references?"

**Why they raise it:** The working group may design criteria that inadvertently lock out smaller specialized vendors and favor Huawei, large Western systems integrators, or CIS primes.

**Response:** "This is exactly why we want to engage during the criteria-drafting phase, not after. Criteria that reward volume of government references over bilingual language coverage or UzCloud data residency will inadvertently favor vendors who don't prioritize Uzbek sovereign AI capability. We can help draft criteria that reward the outcomes PP-320 actually cares about — and that are also internationally verifiable by EBRD and ADB co-financiers."
