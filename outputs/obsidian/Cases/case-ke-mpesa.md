# Kenya M-Pesa Mobile Money Platform

**Origin**: Kenya  •  **Year**: 2007  •  **Sector**: Finance & Fiscal

## Problem solved

Kenya had 80%+ unbanked population but rapidly growing mobile penetration. Traditional banking was too expensive and geographically inaccessible for rural and low-income Kenyans. M-Pesa (Safaricom) created a mobile-phone-based money transfer, payment, and microfinance service accessible on basic feature phones via USSD, requiring no bank account.

## Architecture

Telecom-hosted mobile wallet: Safaricom registered as a payment service provider (not a bank — critical regulatory innovation). M-Pesa wallet linked to SIM card. USSD-based transactions (no data plan needed). Agent network: 200,000+ M-Pesa agents (small shops) act as cash-in/cash-out points. Interoperability: Lipa Na M-Pesa for merchant payments via QR and NFC. Government integration: Kenya Revenue Authority accepts M-Pesa tax payments. World Bank poverty transfer programmes disbursed via M-Pesa. API: M-Pesa Daraja API for business integration.

## UZ transferability (6/10)

UZ smartphone penetration (65%) is higher than Kenya in 2007, reducing the USSD-first requirement. UZ already has UZCARD/HUMO cards and multiple mobile banking apps. M-Pesa's greatest contribution for UZ is the AGENT NETWORK model for cash-in/cash-out at rural post offices (Qishloq Qurilish Bank branches) — enabling digital payment reach in Karakalpakstan and Surkhandarya.

## KG transferability (7/10)

KG has O!Money and Elsom as partial M-Pesa equivalents. The agent network model is the transferable lesson: rural KG (Talas, Naryn) has limited banking but mobile coverage. O!Pay agent network expansion into village-level coverage is the direct application. KG digital som wallet needs agent network for cash-in/cash-out or it will exclude rural populations.
