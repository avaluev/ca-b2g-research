# Bangladesh a2i Telemedicine + Community Clinics

**Origin**: Bangladesh  •  **Year**: 2009  •  **Sector**: Health

## Problem solved

Bangladesh had 14,000+ Union Digital Centres (UDCs) connecting 130M+ rural citizens to government services, but health services required physical travel to district hospitals. The a2i (Access to Information) programme under the Prime Minister's Office piloted telemedicine service at UDCs — placing videoconference-enabled health consultation in rural community centres already trusted by local populations.

## Architecture

Low-cost hub-and-spoke: each UDC equipped with laptop, broadband (3G fallback), and simple vitals equipment (thermometer, BP cuff, pulse oximeter). District hospital provides scheduled telemedicine sessions. Village-level para-doctor (trained local resident) facilitates consultation, inputs vitals, translates for specialist. Digital prescription generated and printed at UDC. DGDA (Directorate General of Drug Administration) maintained drug database for prescription validation. USAID and UNDP co-financed. No complex EHR initially — simplified Google-Forms-based consultation recording.

## UZ transferability (8/10)

UZ has 14,000+ mahalla (neighborhood) offices as existing touchpoints. Deploying telemedicine at mahalla level — leveraging existing community trust infrastructure rather than building new health centres — is the Bangladesh lesson. UZ MoH could run a 200-mahalla pilot in Ferghana Valley for $1-2M. Donor pathway: World Bank Health, ADB, AKDN. Key adaptation: Uzbek-language UI, integration with UZ E-Health not Google Forms.

## KG transferability (8/10)

KG's network of ayil aymak (rural commune) offices provides the same infrastructure as Bangladesh UDCs. Kyrgyz telemedicine from Bishkek National Hospital to Naryn/Talas provinces is the target. AKDN rural connectivity projects create donor-infrastructure alignment. $1-3M pilot deployment at 100 rural ayil aymak centres is achievable. USAID-successor and UNDP both active in KG health.
