# Australia Robodebt Welfare Debt AI (CAUTIONARY)

**Origin**: Australia  •  **Year**: 2016  •  **Sector**: Justice & Rule of Law

## Problem solved

Australian government aimed to recover welfare overpayments by automatically cross-matching welfare recipients' reported income against ATO (tax office) income data. The algorithmic debt-generation system (Robodebt) was supposed to identify discrepancies and issue debt notices automatically, replacing manual review.

## Architecture

Automated data-matching system cross-referencing Centrelink welfare records against ATO annual income data. When discrepancy detected, system automatically generated debt notice (up to $10,000+), reversed burden of proof (citizen must prove they did NOT owe money), and outsourced debt collection to commercial agencies. No human review of individual cases before debt notices issued. Approximately 470,000 unlawful debt notices generated 2016-2019.

## UZ transferability (2/10)

Score 2 = HIGH RISK if replicated. UZ is considering AI in tax compliance and social assistance targeting. Robodebt's pattern — automated debt generation with reversed burden of proof — is tempting for revenue-hungry government agencies. MUST NOT be replicated. Lesson: human review before any algorithmic debt/penalty issuance is non-negotiable. UZ Ombudsman and Ministry of Justice need explicit mandates to prevent this pattern.

## KG transferability (2/10)

Same cautionary rating. KG's weaker legal aid infrastructure (vs Australia's) means Robodebt-style errors would cause MORE harm, not less. Any KG AI-in-welfare or AI-in-tax-enforcement system must have mandatory human review, transparent appeal process, and independent oversight.
