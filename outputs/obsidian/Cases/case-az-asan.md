# ASAN Service / ASAN Imza One-Stop Government Shops

**Origin**: Azerbaijan  •  **Year**: 2012  •  **Sector**: Public Administration & e-Gov

## Problem solved

Azerbaijani citizens navigated corrupt, slow, multi-window government service delivery. Over 40 government agencies each had separate offices with separate queuing, separate documentation requirements, and unofficial payment demands. ASAN Service (Azerbaijani State Agency for Public Service and Social Innovations) created a physical one-stop-shop model where 30+ services from multiple agencies are delivered in one branded, corruption-resistant environment with queue management, biometric verification, and process-tracking.

## Architecture

Hybrid physical-digital model: 20+ ASAN Service centres across Azerbaijan offering branded, corporatised government service delivery. Backend: ASAN Imza mobile digital signature (SIM-based, similar to Estonian Mobile-ID). Digital layer: ASAN Login SSO federated identity. Key elements: queue management system with real-time dashboards, service time KPIs published publicly, mystery shopper programme for quality control. Anti-corruption architecture: no cash at windows, electronic receipts, recorded transactions. ASAN also operates mobile units (converted buses) for rural areas.

## UZ transferability (9/10)

ASAN is the single most politically resonant reference case for UZ's Agency for Public Services (DXA). The 'corporatised, branded, one-stop' model maps directly to UZ's existing ЦГУ (Centres of Government Services) expansion. Cultural/linguistic proximity (both Turkic). Political economy fit: same presidential-modernisation pattern. ASAN Imza tech maps to UZ MyID. Budget: UZ government has already invested in ЦГУ physical centres — ASAN is the upgrade blueprint. UZ can achieve ASAN-level quality at existing centres for $10-20M investment.

## KG transferability (6/10)

ASAN's physical model is aspirational but requires upfront capital that KG struggles to deploy. Mobile units (bus-based) are highly relevant given KG's dispersed rural population. ASAN Imza mobile-ID architecture is replicable for KG at lower cost than physical infrastructure. Political economy: KG's Japarov administration has strong political will for visible citizen-service wins — ASAN branded centres in Bishkek, Osh, Jalal-Abad are feasible pilot targets. UDP would need to drive this directly.
