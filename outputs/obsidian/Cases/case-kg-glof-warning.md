# Kyrgyzstan GLOF (Glacial Lake Outburst Flood) Early Warning System

**Origin**: Kyrgyzstan  •  **Year**: 2019  •  **Sector**: Environment & Climate

## Problem solved

Kyrgyzstan has 2,000+ glacial lakes fed by 8,000+ glaciers in the Tien Shan. Glacial retreat (accelerating at 0.8%/year) is increasing GLOF events — sudden, catastrophic floods from glacial lake outbursts that destroy downstream villages with minutes of warning. The KG Hydromet Service needed automated lake monitoring and early warning to give downstream communities 30+ minutes notice.

## Architecture

UNDP/GEF funded pilot: satellite lake area monitoring (Sentinel-2 time series, lake area growth anomaly detection), 30 high-priority lakes instrumented with GPS-tracked shore sensors + acoustic water level gauges. AI module: lake area + inflow rate change detection triggering alert, downstream flood routing model (using DEM + HydroSHEDS drainage basin data). Alert propagation: SMS alerts to ayil aymak (local government) and village Telegram groups. Integration with KG Hydromet national early warning system.

## UZ transferability (7/10)

UZ Ferghana Valley receives floodwaters from KG mountain GLOF events — UZ is a downstream stakeholder in KG's GLOF warning system. Bilateral data sharing: KG GLOF alerts should automatically trigger UZ downstream alert for Ferghana communities. Budget: $1-2M for UZ downstream alert integration with KG GLOF system. World Bank DRM project funding.

## KG transferability (9/10)

This IS a KG case — Phase 2 expansion to 100 lakes is the ACTIVE PROCUREMENT. The Phase 2 sensor network, satellite monitoring platform, and community alert system expansion is a $15-20M procurement. GEF, UNDP, World Bank DRM project are potential funders. This is one of the most urgent and directly actionable cases in the KG portfolio.
