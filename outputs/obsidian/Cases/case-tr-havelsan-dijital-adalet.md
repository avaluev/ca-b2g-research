# Havelsan Digital Judiciary Platform (Turkey)

**Origin**: Turkey  •  **Year**: 2015  •  **Sector**: Justice & Rule of Law

## Problem solved

Turkey's judiciary processed 6M+ cases annually with paper-based filing, manual case assignment, and physical document storage requiring warehouse-scale facilities. UYAP (National Judicial Network Project) had digitised case filing by 2015 but lacked AI-assisted workflow automation, predictive docketing, and citizen-facing case tracking. Havelsan (state-owned defence and cybersecurity company) developed an AI layer on top of UYAP to automate case routing, detect similar precedents, and assist prosecutors with document review.

## Architecture

AI overlay on UYAP case management system. Three modules: (1) Smart Case Routing — ML classifier assigns incoming cases to judicial chambers based on case type, complexity, and judicial load balancing; (2) Precedent Search — semantic similarity search across 30M+ Turkish court decisions using BERT-Turkish model; (3) Prosecutor Workbench — document summarisation and evidence classification for criminal prosecution files. On-premises deployment within Ministry of Justice secure network. Havelsan provides system integration; Turkish universities provided NLP models.

## UZ transferability (9/10)

Havelsan is the most commercially-realistic vendor option for UZ УП-140 Digital Court programme. Turkish-UZ relations are strong (Türkiye consistently in top-3 UZ trade partners). Havelsan has active presence in UZ through ASELSAN civil products. Russian + Uzbek NLP capability exists or can be developed. Budget: $18M full deployment is within range for UZ Ministry of Justice + potential UNDP co-financing. Direct vendor engagement pathway exists.

## KG transferability (5/10)

Havelsan platform is oversized for KG (KG judiciary is 10x smaller than Turkey). Core concept — AI case routing and precedent search — is highly relevant. The adaptation path would be a scaled-down version, possibly through a Havelsan partner or a local implementation. GIZ digital justice programme in KG could pilot the precedent-search module as standalone tool. Budget concern: $18M full platform is unaffordable; $2-3M module deployment is feasible.
