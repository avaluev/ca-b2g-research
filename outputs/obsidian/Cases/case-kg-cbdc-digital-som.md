# Kyrgyzstan Digital Som CBDC (In Progress — Law Enacted 2025)

**Origin**: Kyrgyzstan  •  **Year**: 2022  •  **Sector**: Finance & Fiscal

## Problem solved

KG has high remittance dependency ($3B+/year, 25% of GDP) with expensive and slow cross-border transfers via Western Union/MoneyGram, and significant unbanked rural population (45% of adults). Digital som (CBDC) could reduce remittance cost, provide government transfer efficiency for pensions/social payments, and extend financial inclusion to rural populations with only mobile phone access.

## Architecture

NBKR design in progress (as of May 2026): two-tier CBDC — NBKR issues digital som, commercial banks and licensed payment operators distribute wallets to consumers. Technical design: blockchain or distributed ledger for settlement (specific technology TBD based on BIS/IMF advisory). Wallet types: full KYC (higher limits), simplified KYC (lower limits), offline-capable wallet for rural areas. Programmable payments: conditioned transfers for social benefits. Cross-border: proposed integration with KZ digital tenge (when available) and EAEU payment rails. Legal basis: Law on CBDC amendments to National Bank Law (April 2025) and USDKG stablecoin law (2025). Full CBDC in force: planned January 2027.

## UZ transferability (7/10)

UZ CBU is monitoring KG digital som as a nearby precedent. If KG's CBDC succeeds, UZ will face political pressure to implement equivalent. Lessons from KG digital som design will directly inform UZ CBU's future CBDC architecture. Score 7: UZ is 2-3 years behind KG in CBDC trajectory.

This IS the KG case — it is in procurement. The wallet vendor selection ($5-10M for core infrastructure), government transfer integration ($3-5M), and cross-border remittance API ($2-3M) are all active opportunities in 2026-2027 window.
