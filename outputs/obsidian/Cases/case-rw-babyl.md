# Rwanda Babyl AI Health Chatbot

**Origin**: Rwanda  •  **Year**: 2016  •  **Sector**: Health

## Problem solved

Rwanda Ministry of Health needed to extend primary healthcare access to rural populations with 1 doctor per 6,700 citizens. Babyl (later rebranded as Aluna Health) deployed an AI-powered symptom checker and triage chatbot accessible via feature phones (USSD) and smartphones, directing patients to appropriate care level — self-care, community health worker, clinic, or hospital emergency.

## Architecture

Multi-channel clinical AI: USSD (feature phone, no data plan required), WhatsApp, web, and app. Clinical AI: validated symptom-checking decision tree augmented with ML triage model trained on 100,000+ consultations in Rwanda. Nurse call-back service integrated for medium-risk cases. Integration with Rwanda health insurance (RSSB) for subsidised consultations. Appointment booking for clinic visits. Backend: AWS with Rwanda data residency agreement. Regulatory approval: Rwanda FDA cleared AI as Class II medical device.

## UZ transferability (7/10)

UZ smartphone penetration is 65% — USSD fallback not as critical as Rwanda but still relevant for rural Karakalpakstan. AI triage chatbot in Russian + Uzbek for EPIGU integration is the target application. Key lesson: government subsidy model is essential for sustainability; commercial model won't work. MoH could integrate as a feature in existing EPIGU app. Budget: $2-4M deployment leveraging existing EPIGU infrastructure.

## KG transferability (7/10)

KG smartphone penetration is 70% but rural Batken/Osh have feature phone populations. USSD-based health triage is directly relevant. Russian + Kyrgyz language triage chatbot via WhatsApp or USSD is technically feasible in 12 months. MHIF (Mandatory Health Insurance Fund) integration for subsidised consultations is the business model. Donor pathway: USAID-successor, WHO.
