# Kazakhstan AIFC Astana International Financial Centre Digital Regime

**Origin**: Kazakhstan  •  **Year**: 2018  •  **Sector**: Finance & Fiscal

## Problem solved

Kazakhstan needed to attract global financial services companies and become a regional financial hub, but its Soviet-inherited civil code (Russian law) was unfamiliar and unattractive to international investors. AIFC created an English-language common-law jurisdiction within Kazakhstan, operated by an independent financial regulator and an English-language court (AIFC Court based on English common law), creating a credible alternative regulatory environment for fintech and financial services.

## Architecture

Special legal and regulatory zone: AIFC Act (2015) established a special economic zone with English common law jurisdiction, independent AIFC Court and AIFC International Arbitration Centre, AIFC Financial Services Regulation (modelled on FCA/DFSA standards). Digital regime components: FinTech Lab regulatory sandbox (100+ fintech companies licensed); AIFC Digital Assets Regulation (crypto exchange licensing); AIFC Green Finance Centre; digital-only bank licensing framework. Physical infrastructure: Expo 2017 site (now AIFC business centre). AI components: AIFC AI Center for financial AI research and standards.

## UZ transferability (7/10)

UZ's IT Park operates as a tech SEZ but lacks the financial services regulatory sandbox equivalent of AIFC. Creating an AIFC-equivalent financial innovation zone within UZ IT Park would enable: digital-only bank licensing, fintech sandbox, Islamic finance innovation. Presidential economic reform agenda supports this. Budget: $20-40M for regulatory infrastructure (not physical infrastructure). Key: requires Central Bank support for sandbox licensing.

## KG transferability (5/10)

KG HTP already has a limited regulatory sandbox. AIFC-level investment ($400M+) is far beyond KG. But the key AIFC principle — common law contracts + independent arbitration for fintech companies — is achievable for KG at much lower cost via bilateral treaty with an established arbitration centre (UNCITRAL, Vienna). Score 5: high concept relevance, low full-model transferability.
