# Kazakhstan e-Court System (Torelik)

**Origin**: Kazakhstan  •  **Year**: 2013  •  **Sector**: Justice & Rule of Law

## Problem solved

Kazakhstan courts received 1.5M+ cases annually with paper-based filing, physical hearings requiring participant travel, and no public access to case status or decisions. Corruption at service windows in courts was documented. The government needed to digitise the full court lifecycle — filing, assignment, hearing, decision, archive — and provide citizens with remote access to court proceedings.

## Architecture

Torelik (Supreme Court digital platform) integrates: online case filing (all case types via torelik.gov.kz), e-signature on all documents (via KZ NCA eSign), video hearing system (integration with Cisco WebEx + local video bridges), automated case assignment algorithm (workload-based, randomised to reduce corruption), open court decision database. Integration with notary registry, civil registry, and enforcement services. Mobile app for case tracking. AI module: legal document similarity search added 2022 using KZ-language BERT models.

## UZ transferability (9/10)

Torelik is the most directly transferable justice-sector case for UZ. Kazakhstan's court system is the closest regional analog to UZ's — same Soviet procedural law foundation, same Russian-language court documentation, same backlog patterns. UZ УП-140 'Digital Court' programme is explicitly building a Torelik-equivalent. Key lessons: randomised automated case assignment is the highest-impact anti-corruption component; prioritise online filing before trying AI features.

## KG transferability (8/10)

KG court system (200,000 cases/year) is smaller than KZ but same procedural DNA. Torelik architecture is directly replicable at 1/5 the cost given KG's smaller scale. KG already has a court information system — Torelik is the upgrade path. Video hearing system is especially relevant given KG's mountainous geography making physical court attendance expensive.
