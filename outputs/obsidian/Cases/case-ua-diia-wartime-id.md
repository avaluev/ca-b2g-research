# Ukraine Diia Digital ID Under Wartime Conditions (Resilience Case)

**Origin**: Ukraine  •  **Year**: 2022  •  **Sector**: Security & Public Order

## Problem solved

Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 created an unprecedented test for digital government: 6M+ IDPs (internally displaced persons) needed to prove identity, access benefits, and register with authorities while physical document offices were destroyed or inaccessible. Diia's digital ID proved to be a critical tool for continuity of civil registration and social payments.

## Architecture

Diia emergency adaptation: (1) Digital Identity — Diia digital passport legally equivalent to physical, accepted at border crossings and police checkpoints; (2) IDP Registration — digital registration of displaced persons at any mobile device with location data; (3) Emergency Payments — direct digital payments (5,000 UAH/person) to Diia-verified citizens bypassing destroyed banking infrastructure; (4) Damage Documentation — citizens photograph destroyed property via Diia for insurance/reparations processing; (5) Wartime Certificate — digital military service exemptions and veteran status. All served via AWS GovCloud with European backups. 14M Diia users actively using wartime features.

## UZ transferability (7/10)

UZ faces natural disaster risk (earthquakes in Tashkent, Ferghana Valley; flooding in Aral Sea basin). EPIGU + MyID emergency services module — enabling digital identity verification, benefit payments, and property damage documentation during disasters — is directly applicable. Budget: $2-3M to add disaster-response module to existing EPIGU. World Bank Disaster Risk Management project provides donor pathway.

## KG transferability (8/10)

KG faces significant disaster risk: earthquakes (near Bishkek fault zone), GLOF events, and mudslides. Diia wartime architecture maps to KG peacetime disaster scenarios. The key lesson: IDP/disaster victim digital registration should work OFFLINE for when connectivity is disrupted. Budget: $1.5-2.5M disaster-resilience module for KG Tunduk services. World Bank disaster risk project is the donor pathway.
