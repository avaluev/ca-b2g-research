# Sidewalk Labs Toronto Smart City (CANCELLED) — CAUTIONARY

**Origin**: Canada  •  **Year**: 2017  •  **Sector**: Transport & Urban

## Problem solved

Toronto Waterfront Authority sought a developer for Quayside, an underdeveloped waterfront district. Sidewalk Labs (Google/Alphabet subsidiary) proposed an AI-driven smart city featuring autonomous vehicles, wood-frame high-rise buildings, underground delivery robots, and comprehensive sensor network — building the 'city of the future.'

## Architecture

Proposed (never built): underground autonomous tunnel delivery system, digital civic grid (sensors throughout public spaces), AI traffic management, dynamic sidewalk surfaces, data trust governance for citizen data. Platform: Sidewalk's proprietary data governance framework. Revenue model: Sidewalk Labs would capture data value in exchange for infrastructure investment. Cancelled May 2020 due to: (1) COVID-19 financial uncertainty; (2) data governance concerns; (3) Alphabet subsidiary business model conflict with public interest; (4) community opposition.

## UZ transferability (2/10)

Score 2 = CAUTIONARY. UZ's Tashkent Smart City initiative should learn this failure pattern. Key: any smart city data infrastructure must be government-owned (not vendor-owned), with citizen data governance framework in place before sensors deploy. Specifically relevant to avoiding Alibaba or Huawei platform lock-in in Tashkent smart city.

## KG transferability (2/10)

Same cautionary rating. KG smart city projects should avoid the big-tech-as-data-beneficiary model. Government as data owner, vendor as service provider.
