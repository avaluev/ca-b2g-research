# Kazakhstan AI for Critical Minerals Exploration (Kazakhmys/Altyntau)

**Origin**: Kazakhstan  •  **Year**: 2020  •  **Sector**: Environment & Climate

## Problem solved

Kazakhstan holds 25% of world's chromite, 30% of manganese, and significant copper/gold deposits. Manual geological survey data interpretation required PhD geophysicists taking 6-18 months per prospect. AI-assisted mineral potential mapping using satellite imagery, historical geological survey data, and geophysical readings could reduce exploration cycle from 5 years to 18 months.

## Architecture

KazGeology AI platform (MIID — Ministry of Industry and Infrastructural Development): satellite multispectral analysis (Sentinel-2 + ASTER thermal) for surface mineralogy mapping; historical Soviet geological survey data digitisation (30,000+ reports scanned via OCR + NLP extraction); machine learning mineral potential model (random forest on geochemical + geophysical + remote sensing features). Commercial application: Kazakhmys Corporation using platform to prioritise 50+ exploration blocks. KazGeology also provides academic access to model outputs for 7 Central Asian universities.

## UZ transferability (8/10)

UZ has gold, uranium, copper, molybdenum, and rare earth deposits with significant unexplored potential. Soviet geological archive at GIGEOS (State Institute of Geology) has same structure as Kazakhstan's. AI mineral exploration using free satellite data + digitised Soviet archives is achievable for $3-6M. US DFC (critical minerals financing), EBRD, and ADB are active funders in UZ mining sector.

## KG transferability (8/10)

KG has gold (Kumtor and 50+ unexplored deposits), antimony, and rare earths. Soviet geological archive in KG Geological Survey. AI mineral exploration is HIGH PRIORITY under KG's economic strategy. US DFC has specific critical minerals programme for KG. Budget: $3-5M. This is a convergent case: mining + digital + export revenue potential.
