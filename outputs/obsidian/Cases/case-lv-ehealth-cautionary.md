# Latvia eHealth (Multi-Restart Programme) — CAUTIONARY

**Origin**: Latvia  •  **Year**: 2009  •  **Sector**: Health

## Problem solved

Latvia attempted to replicate Estonia's e-Health success. Similar problem statement: fragmented hospital IT, paper prescriptions, no national patient record. Latvia had access to Estonia's architecture blueprints and shared X-Road integration layer. Should have been a straightforward replication — it became a 13-year saga of failed procurements and restarts.

## Architecture

Programme attempted: centralised national health portal, e-prescription system, EHR integration via eVeseliba portal. Reality: three complete procurement failures (2009, 2014, 2018). Final 2020 restart used more modular approach: e-prescription only first (live 2021), patient portal second (partial live 2022). Total systems: Tieto (Finnish vendor) won 2020 tender for e-prescription, NIC Latvia (in-house) built patient portal. X-Road integration was available but agencies refused to connect.

## UZ transferability (2/10)

Score 2 = CAUTIONARY CASE. UZ E-Health must avoid the Latvia pattern. Specific risks for UZ: (1) over-scoped initial procurement (build everything at once); (2) no TEHIK-equivalent implementation unit with technical authority; (3) international vendor promises without local capacity building; (4) hospital sector resistance from existing software vendors (multiple Russian МИС vendors are embedded in UZ hospitals). UZ must adopt Estonia's modular approach: e-prescription FIRST, patient portal SECOND.

## KG transferability (2/10)

Same cautionary rating. KG additionally at risk due to smaller government IT capacity. Key protection: ensure Tunduk (GP Infocom) has mandate as the integration hub, avoiding the Latvia problem of agencies refusing to connect. Scope strictly to e-prescription + basic EHR in Phase 1.
