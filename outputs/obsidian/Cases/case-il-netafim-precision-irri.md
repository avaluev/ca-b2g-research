# Israel Netafim Precision Irrigation AI Export

**Origin**: Israel  •  **Year**: 1965  •  **Sector**: Agriculture & Water

## Problem solved

Central Asia faces a critical water challenge: the Aral Sea basin, which serves UZ and KG, has been massively depleted by Soviet-era flood irrigation. Cotton and wheat irrigation in UZ uses 3-5x more water per hectare than equivalent drip irrigation technology. Netafim's precision drip irrigation systems — originally developed for Israel's Negev desert — reduce agricultural water use by 50-70% while maintaining or improving yields.

## Architecture

Hardware + software system: subsurface drip irrigation emitters (Netafim patented anti-clogging technology), soil moisture sensor network, evapotranspiration model-driven irrigation scheduling. Digital layer: NetBeat cloud-based platform integrating sensor readings, weather forecast, crop water demand models, and remote control of irrigation valves. AI: crop-specific irrigation recommendations, anomaly detection (pipe leak, clogging), yield correlation analytics. Deployed via local agriculture ministry partnerships or private distributor networks. In CA context: deployed with UNDP/World Bank water projects in UZ pilot canals.

## UZ transferability (8/10)

UZ President Mirziyoyev has publicly committed to drip irrigation expansion (20% of irrigated land by 2030 target). World Bank water sector projects include drip irrigation components. The AI/digital monitoring layer is where procurement opportunity is largest: UZ needs sensors, IoT platform, and soil moisture analytics — the hardware can be domestic or Chinese substitute, the data platform is the procurement object. Budget: $15-25M government subsidy programme for 200,000 ha expansion is within UZ financial capacity.

## KG transferability (6/10)

KG irrigated agriculture is smaller (700,000 ha vs UZ 4.5M ha) and more mountainous. Drip irrigation technology is relevant but the market size is limited. The AI monitoring platform is useful at lower scale. Key application in KG: greenhouse/horticulture sector (growing rapidly in Chui Valley) rather than field crops. Budget: $3-5M UNDP water project component.
