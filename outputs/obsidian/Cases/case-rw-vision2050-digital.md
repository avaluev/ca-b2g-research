# Rwanda Vision 2050 AI National Strategy

**Origin**: Rwanda  •  **Year**: 2020  •  **Sector**: Cross-cutting

## Problem solved

Rwanda, with a population of 13.5M and GDP of $12B, needed to demonstrate that a small, low-income African country could deploy AI at scale in public services. Vision 2050 included AI as a transformative pillar — not for status signalling but to specifically address Rwanda's infrastructure bottlenecks: healthcare access, agricultural productivity, and government service delivery in a mountainous country.

## Architecture

Rwanda AI Policy 2023: government AI use case registry (50+ approved applications across ministries), National AI Lab (University of Rwanda AI Center), Smart Rwanda Master Plan 2024-2029 including AI infrastructure (400GPU compute available by 2025). Applications in production: Babyl health triage, Irembo digital services (see separate cases), agriculture drone monitoring (crop disease detection via mobile image), e-court pilot (AI transcription). Governance: Rwanda Utilities Regulatory Authority AI ethics committee. Rwanda positioning: 'AI-first small country' as export pitch for other low-income government AI programmes.

## UZ transferability (6/10)

Rwanda is a smaller-economy reference point. Key lesson for UZ: government AI use case registry (50+ approved applications) — UZ MoDT should create a formal registry as governance mechanism. Rwanda's AI ethics committee approach is directly applicable to UZ's AI governance gap.

## KG transferability (8/10)

Rwanda (13.5M, $12B GDP) is the closest population and income comparator to KG (6.5M, $12B GDP). Rwanda's three-year progression from 0 to 50+ government AI applications on small budget ($85M over 5 years) is exactly what KG's AI strategy should target. Rwanda-KG South-South learning programme (via UNDP or World Bank) would be high-value.
