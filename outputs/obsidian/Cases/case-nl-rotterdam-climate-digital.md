# Rotterdam Climate Adaptation Digital Twin

**Origin**: Netherlands  •  **Year**: 2018  •  **Sector**: Environment & Climate

## Problem solved

Rotterdam (90% below sea level) needed to plan and monitor climate adaptation infrastructure (green roofs, water squares, permeable pavements, underground storage) across the city. Different departments maintained separate data on infrastructure, land use, and climate risk with no integrated view for planning or emergency response.

## Architecture

Rotterdam Climate Digital Twin: FIWARE (open-source IoT platform) aggregating sensor data from 200+ rain gauges, 500+ water level sensors, 1,200+ environmental sensors. 3D city model (CityGML format) with overlay of flood risk zones, green infrastructure coverage, and heat island intensity. AI modules: flash flood prediction (4-hour advance warning, ML on sensor + NWP data), urban heat island mapping (ML on temperature sensors + satellite), green infrastructure performance measurement. Open data portal: all sensor data publicly accessible. Policy application: developers must show digital twin impact assessment for new buildings.

## UZ transferability (7/10)

Tashkent summer temperatures (45°C peak in 2024) create urban heat crisis. Flash floods in Ferghana Valley are a seasonal hazard. Rotterdam's sensor network + flash flood warning is directly applicable. Start with flash flood early warning (highest urgency) then add heat island mapping. Budget: $5-8M for Tashkent urban climate monitoring pilot. Donor pathway: ADB urban, World Bank DRM, GEF.

## KG transferability (7/10)

Bishkek faces both flash flooding (debris flows from Ala-Too mountains) and heat island effects. Rotterdam's architecture is applicable at Bishkek city scale for $4-6M. The mountain-city flash flood model (different dynamics from Rotterdam sea-level flooding) requires adaptation. UNDP urban resilience programmes are donor pathways.
