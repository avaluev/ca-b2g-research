## Executive Summary

The 2025-2026 period marks a structural inflection in Central Asian B2G AI markets. Two mutually reinforcing dynamics — legislative activation and vendor vacuum — have created the highest-density opportunity window in the region's post-Soviet digital history.

In Uzbekistan, three presidential decrees signed in the seven weeks between August and October 2025 (УП-140, УП-189, ПП-320) committed $100M in Reconstruction Fund money to AI, mandated 100 AI projects by end-2026, and launched a comprehensive judiciary AI program. The first AI-specific law (ЗРУ-1115, January 2026) followed, creating regulatory clarity that previously blocked enterprise AI procurement. The combination — budget, mandate, and legal framework arriving simultaneously — is unprecedented.

In Kyrgyzstan, the April 30 2026 liquidation of the Ministry of Digital Development and consolidation of all digital functions under the Presidential Administration represents the most consequential single institutional event in KG's digital governance history. Every existing vendor relationship is disrupted. Every program TOR must be redrafted. Simultaneously, the Digital Code of KR (in force February 2026) creates new compliance-driven procurement demand across AI, data processing, and cybersecurity.

The vendor vacuum across both markets is acute and widening. Russian IT vendors face geopolitical risk for their CA clients. Western vendors (Oracle, SAP, IBM) never established deep CA government penetration and are now deprioritizing the region. Chinese vendors (Huawei, ZTE, Alibaba Cloud) are active but face data sovereignty concerns in justice and health domains. The gap between CA government AI ambition and available qualified vendors has never been wider.

## Part I: Uzbekistan Trend Analysis

### 1.1 The Three-Decree Cascade: October 2025 Changed Everything

Understanding the Uzbekistan AI market requires grasping why October 2025 was not a single policy announcement but a cascade: three interconnected decrees creating a self-reinforcing implementation machine.

**ПП-358 (October 14, 2024)** — The AI Strategy 2030 — provided the strategic framework: $1.5B revenue target, Government AI Readiness Top-50 target, Center for AI and Digital Economy created, priority sectors named. This was the announcement-stage decree that most analysts noticed and most vendors addressed.

**УП-189 (October 22, 2025)** — "Additional Measures for AI Development" — was the operational cascade. This decree gave the AI Strategy teeth: 100 AI projects by end-2026, 15 AI labs by end-2026, AI in school curricula from 2026/2027, weather forecasting AI by March 2026, language corpus by end-2026, and critically — $100M from the Reconstruction and Development Fund. This is the implementation decree that creates actual procurement demand.

**ПП-320 (October 30, 2025)** — Just 8 days after УП-189 — was the funding mechanism: $50M in state-body AI grants (distributed by Coordination Commission), $50M equity ($25M IT Park Ventures + $25M Fund of Funds). This decree is currently in active distribution planning. The criteria for the $50M state-body grant distribution are **not yet finalized** as of May 2026. This is the single highest-leverage moment for vendor positioning in Uzbekistan's AI market history.

The cascade means: Strategy (ПП-358) → Mandate (УП-189) → Funding (ПП-320) → Legal Framework (ЗРУ-1115) arrived as a tight cluster. Any vendor who engaged only with ПП-358 (the 2024 strategy) and missed the October 2025 cascade has already lost positioning time.

### 1.2 Public Administration: The Service Digitalization Flywheel

Uzbekistan's EPIGU (Единый интерактивный портал государственных услуг) now hosts 720+ government services. ПП-162 (2023) set a 70% electronic government services target by end-2025, a target being extended and deepened by ПП-286 (September 2025) with 41 priority digital projects. The mahalla-level digital government program — bringing 30+ service types to community centers — creates a last-mile delivery challenge that AI can address.

The maturity assessment for AI chatbots on EPIGU is **accelerating** rather than emerging because the infrastructure is already in place. UZINFOCOM has integrated 720+ services; the bottleneck is citizen-facing AI that can navigate service complexity in Uzbek. This is a solved technical problem globally (Estonian X-Road AI layers, Indian DigiYatra) — it needs Uzbek-language NLP.

Critical observation: UZINFOCOM is the state integrator for virtually every government IT system in Uzbekistan. Any AI vendor seeking government deployment must either work with UZINFOCOM or work around them. This creates both a challenge (UZINFOCOM can block competition) and an opportunity (UZINFOCOM needs AI subcontractors they don't have internally).

**Implementation-stage flag**: ПП-286 is currently being implemented — budget allocated, agencies have targets, procurement is live. This is not announcement-stage.

### 1.3 Justice & Rule of Law: УП-140 is the Clearest Entry Point

Among all trends identified, the judiciary AI program under УП-140 (August 2025) has the most specific procurement signals:

- **Named deliverables** with specific dates: legal analysis platform (Dec 1, 2025), judicial act generation (end-2025), virtual legal consultant (Jan 1, 2026), nationwide rollout (2026-2027)
- **Named integrator**: UZINFOCOM — creating a defined procurement pathway
- **Named institution**: Supreme Court's new Department of Digital Technologies and AI

The Dec 2025/Jan 2026 milestones have already been partially missed (given the decree date of August 2025 and the complexity of building judicial AI). This means procurement is either complete (incumbent won) or still open. Investigating UZINFOCOM's judiciary AI project status is the immediate action.

Turkish vendor Havelsan is the competitive reference explicitly named in Uzbek government documents reviewing digital judiciary options. Havelsan's E-Judiciary Platform is deployed in Turkey and marketed to Turkic-language jurisdictions. A CIS-positioned alternative with Uzbek-language NLP capability competes directly.

### 1.4 Health: Three Decrees, One Integration Layer, Multiple Entry Points

The health AI market in Uzbekistan has unusually clear procurement structure because UZINFOCOM was designated the single integrator for Electronic Healthcare under ПП-415 (2023), and every subsequent health decree (УП-88 May 2025, ПП-311 Sep 2024) builds on this layer.

The introduction of state medical insurance (ПП-311, effective Jan 2025 for digital processing) creates an entirely new budget source for AI: insurance fund fraud detection. Unlike general government AI grants which compete across sectors, insurance fraud detection AI is paid from insurance premiums — a separate funding stream with no competition from other AI projects.

The **e-prescription fraud** use case is particularly attractive: it is technically well-defined (supervised ML on prescription pattern anomalies), has a clear buyer (insurance fund), has clear ROI measurement (fraud loss reduction), and has a natural pilot scope (one pharmacy network or one region). World Bank and UNICEF co-financing requirements create standards compatibility needs that favor non-Russian vendors.

### 1.5 Education: 2026 Window is Now

The school AI curriculum rollout mandated for academic year 2026/2027 under УП-189 creates a procurement deadline that cannot slip: school years have fixed start dates. The 15 AI laboratory mandate with an end-2026 deadline is similarly fixed. These are not "eventually someday" projects — they are being procured now.

The KOICA $14M "Enhancing Youth Employment" program creates a parallel pathway. KOICA programs have more accessible procurement than World Bank projects — they accept concept notes and direct engagement from Korean and international vendors. Korean EdTech companies (Mathpresso, Elice, codeit) have proven CA government engagement via KOICA.

The teacher training component is systematically underserved. Everyone focuses on the student-facing AI platform, but 500K+ teachers need professional development before curriculum 2026/2027 launches. This is a mass-scale, less technically complex, faster procurement than the underlying AI tutoring platform.

### 1.6 Agriculture: Proven Tech, Political Priority, Donor Money

Cotton and wheat dominate Uzbekistan's agricultural export economy. Both are explicitly named in ПП-257 as targets for digital agriculture. The Agroplatforma integration with satellite remote sensing creates a state-owned data infrastructure that vendors can build analytics on top of.

The Aral Sea crisis creates an existential motivation for water-efficient agriculture AI. The $35M World Bank Geospatial Data Project (Cadastre Agency) explicitly includes satellite imagery for agricultural monitoring. This project creates a 5-year procurement pipeline with annual tender tranches — more predictable than one-time AI grant competitions.

The **precision agriculture TAM estimate of $20M (2025-2026)** is based on: (1) World Bank $35M project with ~35% earmarked for imagery analysis services; (2) Ministry of Agriculture annual digitalization budget estimated at $8-12M based on comparable-scale regional governments; (3) ADB water management program digital components. This is conservative and does not include private sector agritech market.

### 1.7 Finance & Fiscal: Shadow Economy Creates Mandate for AI

Uzbekistan's shadow economy estimated at 40-50% of GDP by IMF (2023 Article IV) creates an existential motivation for tax AI that no policy paper can match. The GNK has institutional interest in AI because increased tax collection directly funds GNK's own budget and justifies headcount.

The customs dimension is amplified by Middle Corridor growth. Uzbekistan processed $18B+ in trade in 2024 with trade volumes growing 15%+ annually. Each percentage improvement in customs risk profiling accuracy translates to tens of millions in duty recovery. The EU C4CA customs facilitation component provides a donor-funded entry that avoids the political complexity of direct state budget negotiations.

The AML/anti-money laundering market for state-owned banks is the highest-margin opportunity in the finance sector. State banks collectively hold 80%+ of Uzbek banking assets. They need Western-correspondent-banking-compatible AML tools but cannot use US-listed software (sanctions exposure risk for their clients). This is precisely the Russian/CIS substitution moat opportunity.

## Part II: Kyrgyzstan Trend Analysis

### 2.1 The April 2026 Event: Understanding the Japarov Concentration in Digital

On approximately April 30, 2026, President Sadyr Japarov signed a decree liquidating the Ministry of Digital Development and Innovative Technologies of Kyrgyzstan (Минцифры КР) and transferring all its functions to the Presidential Administration (Управление делами Президента, UDP). The decree created three new structural units within UDP and gave a 1-month deadline for structural regulations and 2-month deadline for aligning all Cabinet decisions.

This event must be understood through the Japarov Concentration lens: since 2021, Japarov has systematically moved decision-making from ministerial to Presidential Administration control for strategic sectors. Digital technology now joins defense, security, and extractive industry as sectors managed directly by the Presidential Administration rather than through the ministerial hierarchy.

The operational implication for vendors is profound: every ongoing project, every donor program counterpart, every procurement relationship that existed through the Ministry of Digital Development is now disrupted. The incoming UDP Digital Department head is not yet named (as of early May 2026). They will arrive at an institution that has absorbed 400+ Ministry employees with unclear portfolios, and will need external technical help to understand what they just inherited.

This is the highest-leverage vendor positioning window in KG digital history since the Tunduk system was created in 2018.

### 2.2 The Digital Code: KG's Most Significant Digital Legislation

The Digital Code of KR (Law 178, July 31 2025, in force February 6 2026) is not an incremental amendment — it is a comprehensive codification of Kyrgyzstan's digital legal framework in a single omnibus law. For AI vendors, Chapter 23 is the critical provision: high-risk AI systems require a "danger assessment" and "compliance confirmation" before deployment.

The Chapter 23 bylaws implementing these requirements are being drafted in 2026. The danger assessment methodology does not yet have a published standard. This is the CW-007 opportunity: the organization that develops and proposes the methodology becomes the de facto standard-setter for all future AI deployments in KG.

The Digital Code also mandates Tunduk as the national data exchange ecosystem backbone (Section IV), creates sectoral regulators for personal data and telecommunications, and requires all existing government information systems to achieve Digital Code compliance by year-end 2026. This creates immediate compliance procurement demand across 50+ government information systems.

### 2.3 Tunduk: Orphaned Asset, Maximum Leverage

Tunduk is technically Kyrgyzstan's X-Road equivalent (forked from Cybernetica's X-Road UXP). It currently connects 50+ government information systems, processes millions of inter-agency data requests, and is the technical backbone for 700+ digital services. It was created under World Bank co-financing and has Estonian EGA institutional involvement.

The April 2026 reorganization moved Tunduk from being a state institution under Mintsifry to being under GP Infocom under UDP. The governance and budget situation is in flux. The World Bank Digital CASA project that co-finances Tunduk modernization now has an uncertain government counterpart.

This creates a narrow but powerful window for vendors with X-Road/UXP technical expertise to offer Tunduk modernization advisory services to the incoming UDP Digital Dept — helping them understand what they inherited while simultaneously positioning for the modernization contract. Estonian EGA is the natural partner for this strategy (they have institutional credibility with both World Bank and the KG government from original Tunduk implementation).

### 2.4 CBDC Digital Som: The Hard-Deadline Procurement

The digital som is the most analytically clear B2G procurement in all of Central Asia for the 2026 horizon. A constitutional law amendment (KG-LAW-2025-074) established January 1, 2027 as the go-live date for the digital som as legal tender. NBKR has exclusive emission rights and operates the platform. A pilot is running in 2026.

The technical requirements are well-defined: wallet platform, AML/KYC AI, fraud detection, transaction monitoring. The legal deadline cannot slip without another constitutional amendment (requiring 2/3 Jogorku Kenesh majority). NBKR is procuring now.

The Russian/CIS substitution lens is particularly sharp here. Russia's digital ruble faces sanctions exposure for KG financial institutions engaging with it. Western payment infrastructure (Visa/Mastercard) has demonstrated it will exit CIS markets under political pressure. Kyrgyzstan wants sovereign payment rails. The digital som is the vehicle. A vendor who can deliver both the CBDC infrastructure AND the virtual asset compliance layer (for the $2B+ annual KG crypto trading volume) creates an integrated financial infrastructure moat that will be very difficult to replace once embedded in NBKR systems.

### 2.5 Virtual Assets: KG as the CIS Crypto Compliance Hub

Kyrgyzstan made an unusual strategic choice: while most CIS jurisdictions either banned or heavily restricted crypto, KG created a licensing framework (Law 12, 2022) and launched a stablecoin (USDKG, October 2025). The National Council on Virtual Assets and Blockchain under the President (Cabinet Resolution, 2025) signals this is a Presidential priority.

The strategic logic is clear: KG wants to capture the regulatory arbitrage opportunity created by Western jurisdictions tightening crypto regulation. If KG can be "the compliant crypto jurisdiction of CIS" — with real AML/KYC infrastructure, FATF Travel Rule compliance, and transparent licensing — it attracts crypto business from both the Russian-side and the Western-side of the regulatory gap.

This creates a genuine market for AI-powered crypto compliance tools. The 30+ licensed KG virtual asset operators need transaction monitoring, AML screening, and FATF compliance tools. Western crypto compliance vendors (Chainalysis, Elliptic) are available but expensive and primarily oriented toward Western regulatory frameworks. A vendor with Russian-language interface, on-chain analytics for USDT/USDKG/BTC transactions common in CIS, and FSA reporting automation is competitively differentiated.

### 2.6 Glacier and Environment: Donor-Funded, Technically Unique, Underprovided

The glacier monitoring AI opportunity in KG is structurally underserved because it requires domain knowledge (Central Asian glaciology) that no major AI vendor has developed. Kyrgyzstan's 7,000+ glaciers are retreating at 0.3-0.7km annually; the Naryn River which powers 40% of KG's electricity comes from these glaciers. Toktogul reservoir management is directly dependent on glacier melt forecasting.

UNDP, ADB, and SDC all have active climate adaptation programs in KG with digital monitoring components. The glacier monitoring use case is at the intersection of three donor programs, requires specialized satellite remote sensing capabilities, and has no dominant incumbent. A vendor who builds this capability first will be the only qualified bidder for the next decade of donor-funded glacier AI contracts across the CAREC region (KG, TJ, KZ, UZ all share Tian Shan glaciers).

## Part III: Cross-Cutting Themes

### 3.1 The Sovereign LLM Race: Why Both Countries Need It Now

Neither Uzbekistan nor Kyrgyzstan can use OpenAI, Google Gemini, or Anthropic APIs for government AI applications because:

1. **Data localization laws** — both countries have personal data laws requiring government data to remain on domestic servers. API calls to foreign cloud providers violate these laws for state data processing.

2. **Language coverage** — Uzbek and Kyrgyz are among OpenAI's lowest-performance languages. GPT-4's Uzbek accuracy is estimated at 60-70% of its English performance for complex legal/medical text.

3. **Explainability requirements** — ЗРУ-1115 (UZ) and Digital Code Chapter 23 (KG) require explainable AI for legally significant decisions. Foreign API black boxes don't satisfy this requirement.

4. **Political sovereignty narrative** — Both governments are building "AI sovereignty" as a political message. Deploying on OpenAI contradicts this narrative.

The race to develop sovereign LLMs — UzLLM and KyrgyzLLM — is already underway. Uzbekistan has the more advanced institutional framework (AI Center, Alliance for AI Development, $90M credit for AI implementation). Kyrgyzstan's smaller scale makes a faster MVP possible (smaller training corpus, simpler government vocabulary).

The key technical insight: Uzbek is a Turkic language with significant overlap with Turkish and Kazakh. Transfer learning from existing multilingual models (mBERT, XLM-R) with Uzbek-specific fine-tuning on the 50B+ token Uzbek government document corpus is technically feasible within 6 months. Kyrgyz similarly benefits from Kazakh language model foundations.

The vendor opportunity: a team that builds the first genuinely capable Uzbek LLM with government domain fine-tuning will dominate government AI applications for 3-5 years, because switching costs (retraining on government data, re-certifying under AI law, rebuilding integrations) are enormous.

### 3.2 Donor Architecture: Who Controls the Real Budget

A systematic misconception among foreign vendors entering CA B2G markets: the formal minister is not the real customer. For most AI/digital programs in KG and many in UZ, the real customer is the donor program TTL (Task Team Leader) or Project Manager.

The governance reality: 60-90% of "government" AI/digital budgets in KG are donor budgets channeled through ministries. The minister decides which programs to prioritize politically; the World Bank TTL decides which activities are technically eligible and how the competitive procurement is structured. Vendors who pitch only to ministers and ignore donor TTLs lose consistently.

The donor map for the two highest-volume programs:
- World Bank Digital CASA (KG): TTL based in Almaty/Washington, counterpart now in UDP
- ADB 55109-001 e-Procurement (KG): Project Officer in Manila, counterpart in KG Cabinet/UDP
- EU C4CA cybersecurity (both): Programme Manager in Brussels, implementer is Estonian EGA

Finding and engaging these individuals is not difficult — they are named in project documents on donor websites — but most vendors never look. The diaspora bridge lens applies here too: Uzbek and Kyrgyz staff at World Bank, ADB, and EU Delegation are warm introduction paths.

### 3.3 Implementation vs. Announcement: The Critical Distinction

Every trend in this report was assessed for implementation vs. announcement maturity using the Decree Half-Life framework:

**Active implementation-stage trends** (where contracts can result within 12 months):
- UZ: Digital Court AI (УП-140), E-Health platform (ПП-415/УП-88), AI grants distribution (ПП-320), Weather AI (УП-189), Cybersecurity Strategy (УП-38)
- KG: UDP TOR drafting (April 2026 decree), Digital Som CBDC (KG-LAW-2025-074), Digital Code secondary regulations, E-Procurement ADB

**Announcement-stage trends** (18-36 month horizon):
- UZ: Smart grid AI, Sovereign LLM full deployment, Aral Basin monitoring
- KG: EHR full rollout, Glacier monitoring full deployment, Kyrgyz LLM

The critical filtering question for every opportunity: "Is there a budget line and a procurement officer assigned?" If yes, it is implementation-stage. If no, it is announcement-stage regardless of how specific the decree language is.

### 3.4 The Russian/CIS Substitution Window is Closing

The post-2022 vendor vacuum that created the Russian/CIS substitution opportunity is not permanent. Several dynamics are converging to narrow it:

1. **Local champions emerging**: UZ IT Park is producing 800+ resident companies, some building government AI tools. Within 2-3 years, local vendors will have enough reference deployments to compete.

2. **Chinese vendor push**: Huawei, ZTE, and Alibaba Cloud are aggressively positioning in both UZ and KG. They have price advantages and relationship capital from Digital Silk Road infrastructure deals.

3. **Kazakh vendors expanding**: Kazakhstan's more advanced digital market is producing vendors (Yandex Kazakhstan, Kaspi, local GovTech companies) that are beginning to expand to UZ/KG.

4. **Indian IT services entry**: TCS, Infosys, and Wipro are establishing CA presence; they have WB/ADB vendor qualification that CIS-local vendors lack.

The window for a non-Russian CIS-aligned AI vendor (Russian-language UX, data sovereignty compliance, no sanctions exposure) to capture market before local champions emerge is approximately 18-24 months. After that, the competitive landscape normalizes.

## Part V: Risk Factors and Failure Modes

### 5.1 Decree Reversal Risk

Central Asian decrees have a half-life. The risk that a decree will be amended, superseded, or de-prioritized is non-zero. The highest-risk decrees for reversal are those tied to specific financial targets (ПП-358's $1.5B revenue target by 2030 is aspirational; if targets are not met in 18 months, the decree may be revised rather than enforced).

The lowest reversal risk decrees are those tied to international obligations (World Bank loan covenants, ADB project agreements, FATF compliance requirements). KG digital som (KG-LAW-2025-074) has a January 2027 legal deadline embedded in a Constitutional law — reversal requires legislative supermajority. This is structurally more stable than a Presidential decree.

### 5.2 Key Person Dependency

Both markets have critical single-point-of-failure decision-makers:

- **UZ**: Minister Sherzod Shermatov is mentioned in decree authorizations for ПП-358, УП-140, and УП-189. His continuation in role is critical for AI agenda continuity.
- **KG**: The incoming UDP Digital Department head (not yet named as of early May 2026) will define the entire KG digital agenda for 2026-2027. His/her identity and priorities are the most important unknown in KG market analysis.

### 5.3 Procurement Opacity

Both countries have formal e-procurement portals (zakupki.uz, zakupki.gov.kg) but informal procurement pathways remain common for AI/digital services classified as "unique" or "consulting." World Bank and ADB programs provide structured procurement through STEP/ADB portals that are more accessible to international vendors. State budget procurements remain significantly harder to monitor and contest.

### 5.4 Data Localization Compliance Complexity

Both UZ and KG have personal data laws requiring government data to remain domestically. For AI vendors, this means:
- No cloud-only delivery models for government data processing
- On-premise or private cloud deployment capability is mandatory
- Training data for government-specific models must be processed inside country
- Cross-border model updates require legal analysis under both ЗРУ-547 (UZ) and KG Digital Code Chapter 11

Vendors who have not designed for on-premise deployment at the outset will face expensive re-engineering to serve CA government contracts.

## Conclusion

The 2025-2026 CA AI/digital B2G market is the most dynamic it has been in the post-Soviet period. Legislative activation, funding commitment, and vendor vacuum have aligned in a 12-24 month window that will not repeat. The markets will normalize once: (1) ПП-320 grants are deployed and incumbents established; (2) UDP/KG new structure stabilizes and new procurement relationships form; (3) local champion vendors accumulate reference deployments.

For AI/digital vendors positioned in the CIS/post-Soviet space with Russian-language capabilities, on-premise deployment experience, and Central Asian institutional contacts — the next 12 months represent the highest return-on-positioning period this region has offered since the early 2010s e-government buildout.

The 61 trends documented in this report collectively represent approximately $424M in addressable procurement spend across UZ and KG over 2025-2026. The 15 convergent windows represent the subset where multiple dynamics align to create actionable engagement opportunities within 6-12 months.
